Foreign Labor Compliance & Iraqi Social Security Law No. 18 of 2023: Corporate & Investor Guide

A comprehensive analytical study of the provisions of the Iraqi Labor Law and the Workers Retirement and Social Security Law, outlining employer and corporate obligations.

The business and investment environment in the Republic of Iraq is promising yet governed by a strict legislative framework requiring foreign and local companies to be fully familiar with Labor Law No. (37) of 2015 and Workers Retirement and Social Security Law No. (18) of 2023. This analysis highlights key legal obligations: 1. Employment Contracts and Legal Drafting: Iraqi legislation mandates that employment contracts be in writing and in two copies in Arabic (with foreign language translations permitted, while the Arabic text prevails in disputes). Contracts must clearly state work nature, wages, working hours, and a probationary period not exceeding three months. 2. Social Security Contributions (A Core Obligation): The active Social Security Law requires employers to enroll all workers (national and foreign) and pay mandatory contributions. Failure to do so leads to heavy financial penalties and legal liabilities affecting work licenses and residency renewals. 3. Foreign Recruitment & Work Permits: Foreign workers are subject to strict controls requiring Ministry of Labor approvals, work permits, and residency visas complying with national employment quotas. 4. Labor Dispute Resolution: Amicable settlement and labor inspection committees are advised before escalating disputes to labor courts to maintain business continuity and corporate reputation. Executive Recommendation: Given procedural complexities and multiple regulatory bodies, we emphasize having all employment contracts and corporate bylaws audited by specialized legal counsel.
